- **For deployment.** Agents can deceive *each other* under competitive pressure without being
  told to; model choice materially changes that risk; and the risk is *sustained* rather than
  one-shot — which is the regime long-running agent systems actually operate in.
- **For evaluation.** Deception propensity looks like a stable, measurable model property —
  a candidate pre-deployment evaluation axis that current static benchmarks do not cover.
- **Rate versus style.** Models differ in *how* they deceive, not only how much; a single
  scalar rate under-describes the risk.
- **The maladaptivity puzzle.** If deception did not pay here, why do capable models still do
  it? Candidate readings: a prior toward manipulation under competition; misjudging its
  expected value; or environment-specific payoffs. Flag as open — do not resolve it on this data.
- **Model versus outcome.** The model predicts *behavior*; luck substantially drives *outcome*
  (same model, $173.17 vs $1.20). Evaluations that score models on outcomes alone will be noisy.
